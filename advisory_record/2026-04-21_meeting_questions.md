## 1. Which shock is more economically compelling — HPR or 棚改货币化?

The 2016 HPR is conceptually clean but CFPS county masking forces a province-level proxy, which produces noisy estimates. 棚改货币化 varies sharply across provinces and its intensity is published at the right granularity. Does the PSL cash → local demand surge → price spike → fertility response chain hold up as a mechanism, or is it too far removed from household decision-making?

## 2. What is the theoretical channel — cost or uncertainty?

Housing prices could depress fertility through: (a) affordability (mortgage burden crowds out child-rearing), (b) option value / uncertainty (couples wait until housing is "settled"), or (c) space constraints. Which channel is most credible in the Chinese context, and can the data distinguish between them? This shapes which heterogeneity cuts are worth running.

## 3. Postponement or permanent reduction?

The event study (TWFE, individual & year FE) shows young women (20–32 in 2016) had a slightly higher birth probability in 2016–2018, then turned negative in 2020–2022 — consistent with a delayed response and possible postponement. Nothing is statistically significant, likely because the province-level treatment is too noisy. 

Key question: does she see this as a tempo effect (births shift later, completed fertility unchanged) or a quantum effect (fewer children over a lifetime)? The distinction matters for policy — a tempo effect partially self-corrects; a quantum effect is a real demographic loss.

## 4. Two-child policy confounder — control or moderator?

The January 2016 two-child policy is concurrent with HPR. Two framings:
- **Confounder**: partial out its effect with a parity control or province × policy interaction.
- **Moderator**: did high housing prices *mute* the fertility response to the two-child liberalisation? This could be an interesting finding — provinces with higher prices may have seen a smaller second-birth response to the policy.

## 5. Is the intergenerational transfer finding worth centering?

Households whose primary borrowing source is parents have persistently lower birth rates (8.5% vs 10.1%). Two interpretations:
- **Channel**: parental capacity exhaustion → children bear more housing cost independently → fertility penalty. If so, this becomes a mechanism section.
- **Confound**: correlated with unobserved household financial strain. If so, it's a control variable.

Her view on which framing is more credible determines the paper's structure.

## 6. Is the province-level proxy defensible?

Given the identification limitation, is the paper still viable with an explicit attenuation bias discussion, or does it need a cleaner treatment? This decides between: (a) commit to province-level HPR, (b) switch to 棚改货币化, or (c) invest time in finding a city-level CFPS crosswalk from existing literature.
