# Citation scale and priority (memo of the advisor's comments, recorded 2026-09-07)

Last time I discussed the framework with my advisor (the meeting at which `framework_memo_20260819.md` was updated), she also commented on the number of citations: about twenty works is reasonable for this paper. There is no need to sweep the whole related literature, but the citations should be tiered. A few works are the ones genuinely discussed at length; the rest are one-sentence citations used for positioning. She named three priorities: first, the original paper (Liu & Zhang 2025, from which both the identification strategy and the "zero" come); second, CDDF (the methodological anchor of the completeness certificate); third, the traditional-culture block (lineage, continuing the family line, son preference, which is the root of the norm index and of the sentence "the effect is created by culture").

The current `02_literature_review.md` cites forty-seven distinct works, clearly too many. Below, following her division, the list is reduced to just over twenty, with a clear statement of which works are removed from the main text. All entries are in the verified list in `essay/literature_wrapup_20260723.md`; this memo only selects among them and adds nothing new.

## Tier 1: discussed at length (eight works)

Each of these deserves two or three sentences or more, and the reader should be able to see from the text our specific relationship to it.

Liu & Zhang (2025) is the starting point of the whole paper: same identification, different question. It should be mentioned in §1 ¶3, §2.1, §2.7 and the §6.1 replication. Chernozhukov, Demirer, Duflo & Fernández-Val (2025, Econometrica) is the source of GATES and the BLP certificate, and is introduced in full only once, in §9.1. The culture block gets three works: Zou, Ma, Ma & Zheng (2026) is the nearest neighbour on lineage culture and fertility, and the text must make clear that our index is based on attitude questions rather than genealogy density, and that it is used as a DID moderator; Greif & Tabellini (2017) gives the concept of the "clan" its place in economics; Zhang (2022, China Economic Review) is the micro evidence on patrilineal descent and fertility, and is the closest to the firstborn-daughter margin. Son preference gets two works: Ebenstein (2010) is the standard citation for missing women and the one-child policy, and Almond, Li & Zhang (2019) is the mirror-image comparison for our point that "income shocks intensify sex selection, cost shocks reduce it", and must be contrasted directly. At the couple level, Doepke & Kindermann (2019): our diff DID is a cost-shock comparative static on their disagreement margin. For quantity–quality, Becker & Lewis (1973): one sentence for the classic plus one sentence "we provide direct evidence on expenditures".

## Tier 2: one-sentence positioning (about ten works)

These appear only once, each supporting one sentence. The two-channel framework for housing and fertility uses Dettling & Kearney (2014) and Lovenheim & Mumford (2013); parity progression as revealed son preference uses Ben-Porath & Welch (1976), with Clark (2000) either merged into the same sentence or dropped; cost constraints and "missing women" use Jayachandran (2017); culture as a moderator uses Fernández & Fogli (2009) and Bau (2021), the latter also showing that policy can move culture; the precedent for data on both spouses' preferences uses Rasul (2008); Chinese evidence on quantity–quality uses Rosenzweig & Zhang (2009); the evolution of the one-child policy in the institutional background uses Zhang (2017, JEP), and the effect of the universal two-child policy uses Zeng & Hesketh (2016); marriage housing and son preference pushing up housing demand uses Wei & Zhang (2011), because what we find is the opposite margin.

## Tier 3: method footnotes (no more than six works)

Method citations appear only where the method is first used. Wild cluster bootstrap uses Cameron, Gelbach & Miller (2008) and MacKinnon & Webb (2018) (the sentence is already written in literature_wrapup §A7); the legitimacy of prognostic-score stratification uses Kent & Hayward (2007), and the warning about endogenous stratification uses Abadie, Chingos & West (2018); the causal forest, as a method discarded in the appendix, uses one entry, Wager & Athey (2018); DML uses Chernozhukov et al. (2018) if the appendix mentions it, otherwise it is not cited.

This gives about twenty-four works in the main text plus footnotes, of which eight are genuinely discussed, matching the proportion she described.

## Removed from the current §2

The principle for removal: the same point is already supported by a stronger work, or the point itself has no corresponding result in the main text. Specifically: Daysal et al. (2021), Liu, Liu & Wang (2023), Liu, Xing & Zhang (2020) (the third, fourth and fifth works on housing and fertility; two are enough for the two-channel framework); Das Gupta et al. (2003), Das Gupta (2007), Almond, Edlund & Milligan (2013) (bookends for the framework of persistent son preference; Ebenstein alone is enough); Guo & Zhang (2007), Li & Zhang (2008), Li & Meng (2013) (each of their points is already covered by stronger citations); Alesina, Giuliano & Nunn (2013), Murphy, Tao & Lu (2011), Hu & Scott (2016), Zhang, Wang & Hou (2024), Chen (2025), JDS 2025 Confucianism (the culture block is reduced from nine works to five, keeping the closest); Ashraf, Field & Lee (2014), Testa, Cavalli & Rosina (2014), Voas (2003), Kindermann & Tertilt (2023) (the couple block keeps D–K and Rasul); Anukriti (2018), Edlund et al. (2013), Ma & Zheng (2026), Young (2019), Athey, Tibshirani & Wager (2019), Knaus, Lechner & Strittmatter (2021), Belloni, Chernozhukov & Hansen (2014). The removed entries remain in literature_wrapup and can be retrieved at any time for the defence or revisions.

## Implications for the writing

§2 is compressed from seven subsections to about five paragraphs, each paragraph covering one literature and ending with one sentence on our position; its length goes from 2,200 words down to 1,200–1,500. The contribution paragraph in §1 ¶8 names only Tier 1 works. Method citations move from §2.6 to §9.1 and the appendix, and §2 no longer has a methods subsection. When this list is implemented in `02_literature_review.md`, the removed citations are deleted from the text along with it, rather than left in uncited.
